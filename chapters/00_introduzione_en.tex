\chapter*{Introduction}

These notes originated as lecture notes for the Mathematical Analysis course of
the Physics degree program at the University of Pisa during the academic years
from 2017/18 to 2024/25.

The notes (as well as the course they refer to) concern the analysis of
functions of a real variable. The topics covered include numerical series and
sequences, recursive sequences, differential calculus, and integral calculus. A
minimal amount of functional analysis is conducted with the aim of considering,
as a final topic, the study of ordinary differential equations. Complex numbers
are introduced from the outset and are used where they can help provide a more
unified and conceptually simpler view of the topics covered (particularly in the
study of power series, in the definition of trigonometric functions, and in the
resolution of linear differential equations).

The notes are extensive, with no attempt at conciseness. The goal is to gather
all those results that cannot always be presented in a detailed and rigorous
manner during lectures. For example, we will find the construction of real
numbers, equivalent definitions of the exponential function and trigonometric
functions (with $e$ and $\pi$). We propose the proof of the fundamental theorem
of algebra, Stirling's formula, Wallis's formula, and the irrationality of $e$
and $\pi$. A formal definition of Landau's symbols, small $o$ and big $O$, is
proposed along with the relevant theorems for handling these expressions. The
same is done for the symbol of indefinite integral. Algebraic results are
discussed that justify algorithms for calculating the primitives of rational
functions and for solving linear differential equations using the similarity
method.

The first chapter, dedicated to formal systems, the construction of numerical
sets, and the definition of elementary functions, aims to provide a mathematical
foundation for all those notions that the student should have already learned in
the school curriculum. Some topics covered in the first chapter may be difficult
to understand as they are very technical: they can nevertheless be skipped as
they deal with concepts that should at least intuitively be already known. In
particular, the axiomatic definition of the set of real numbers is probably very
different from how it is usually presented in most textbooks introducing
mathematical analysis: instead of assuming the field properties (and thus the a
priori existence of the two operations of addition and multiplication), only the
addition operation is considered while multiplication is constructed
consequently. This approach is justified by the observation that the
construction of multiplication is exactly the same construction that is usually
used to construct the operation of exponentiation. Thus, the same abstract
theorem (isomorphism of densely and continuously ordered groups) is used to
construct multiplication, exponential functions, and trigonometric functions.

Figures are not frequent, but many of them have a \emph{QR-code} (a square
formed by a cloud of pixels) that allows access to the figure \emph{online} and
to modify its parameters. On the page
\url{https://paolini.github.io/AnalisiUno/}, all links to interactive figures
are provided.

These notes are made freely available both in PDF format and in source form
\LaTeX{}. One way to support this project and keep it freely available for
everyone is to purchase a printed copy.

The material is constantly evolving and certainly contains errors and
inconsistencies. Any suggestions or comments are welcome!
